TOPIC 02 • NOTES

Mycobacteria

Important acid-fast bacilli studied under Gram-positive bacilli. The notes particularly discuss Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

Important species M. tuberculosis
Special feature Acid-fast bacilli
Important disease Tuberculosis

1. Introduction

Mycobacteria are slender bacilli that may show branching. The notes include:

  • Mycobacterium tuberculosis
  • Mycobacterium bovis
  • Mycobacterium phlei

The notes describe different groups including obligate parasites, opportunistic pathogens and saprophytes.

2. Morphology

  • Mycobacteria are described as straight or slightly curved bacilli.
  • They may occur singly or in groups.
  • Some may show branching.
  • M. bovis is described in the notes as shorter and more straight.
Key morphology:
Slender bacilli → straight/slightly curved → may occur singly or in groups.

3. Acid-Fast Staining

One of the most important features of Mycobacteria is their acid-fast nature.

The notes relate acid-fast staining to the presence of mycolic acid in the bacterial cell wall.

Carbol fuchsin → Acid-fast bacilli retain stain → Resist decolorization

The notes describe the use of carbol fuchsin followed by acid decolorization and alcohol-related decolorization steps in acid-fast staining.

⭐ Exam point: Acid-fastness is a major identifying characteristic of Mycobacteria.

4. Cultural Characteristics

  • Mycobacteria are described as slow-growing organisms.
  • The notes mention that colonies may become visible after approximately 10–15 days.
  • The optimum temperature mentioned is around 37°C.
  • The notes mention sensitivity to toll's ... in the context of growth; this part of the handwritten wording is not completely clear, so it is not expanded here.

Important culture media mentioned in the notes

  • Lowenstein–Jensen medium
  • Dubos' Middlebrook medium
  • Beck's ... medium
  • Laurton / related medium as written in the notes
Remember: Mycobacteria are slow growers, and Lowenstein–Jensen medium is specifically mentioned in the notes.

5. Biochemical Reactions

The handwritten notes describe several tests used in the study/differentiation of Mycobacteria.

Test Point mentioned in notes
Niacin test Mentioned as a test for Mycobacterium differentiation.
Arylsulfatase test Mentioned as a biochemical test for differentiation of Mycobacteria.
Catalase–peroxidase Mentioned as a test useful for differentiating typical Mycobacteria.
Nitrate reduction test Mentioned as positive with Mycobacterium tuberculosis in the notes.

6. Laboratory Diagnosis

A. Specimen Collection

  • Sputum is specifically mentioned in the notes.
  • The notes state that sputum should be collected preferably from the morning specimen.
  • Specimen collection is important for detection of the organism.

B. Microscopy

  • The ZN method (Ziehl–Neelsen method) is mentioned for staining sputum.
  • Acid-fast bacilli can be demonstrated by appropriate acid-fast staining.
  • The notes also mention molecular methods such as PCR.

C. Molecular Methods

  • Molecular methods mentioned include PCR.
  • RFLP is also mentioned in the notes.
Diagnosis flow:
Sputum → ZN staining → Acid-fast bacilli → Culture / tests → Molecular methods

7. Important Exam Points

  • Mycobacteria are acid-fast bacilli.
  • Mycolic acid is associated with acid-fastness.
  • They are generally slow-growing.
  • Lowenstein–Jensen medium is an important culture medium mentioned in the notes.
  • Ziehl–Neelsen staining is important for microscopy.
  • Sputum is an important specimen mentioned for laboratory diagnosis.
  • PCR and RFLP are mentioned under molecular methods.
