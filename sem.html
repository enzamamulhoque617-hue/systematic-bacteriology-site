1. Introduction

Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) is an electron microscope. According to the study notes, electrons scan the specimen surface and produce a 3D-like image.

2. Basic Principle

The basic idea is surface scanning. An electron beam is used, the specimen surface is scanned, and the resulting information produces a 3D-like image.

3. Working Principle

  • SEM is an electron microscope.
  • It uses an electron beam.
  • Electrons scan the surface of the specimen.
  • The scanned surface information produces a 3D-like image.

4. Special Feature

The important feature highlighted in the notes is that SEM scans the specimen surface and produces a 3D-like image.

5. Light vs Electron Microscope

  • Light microscope: visible light.
  • Electron microscope: electron beam.
  • SEM: scans the specimen surface.
  • SEM image: 3D-like appearance.

6. Applications

The uploaded notes specifically emphasize SEM for examining the surface of a specimen and obtaining a 3D-like image. Detailed clinical applications are not specified in the uploaded notes, so they are not added here.
