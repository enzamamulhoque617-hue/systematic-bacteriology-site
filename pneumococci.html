1. Morphology

  • Pneumococci are Gram-positive bacteria.
  • They are small cocci, approximately 1–2 µm in size.
  • Cells are slightly elongated.
  • The ends are somewhat pointed, giving a characteristic lancet-like appearance.
  • They occur mainly in pairs.
  • They possess a prominent capsule.
  • They are non-motile.
  • They are non-spore-forming.
Remember: Pneumococcus → small, slightly elongated Gram-positive diplococci with a capsule.

2. Cultural Characteristics

The PDF notes describe pneumococci as having comparatively complex growth requirements.

Growth requirements

  • Growth is described around 37°C.
  • The notes give an approximate pH of 7.2–7.8.
  • Growth is favoured in an atmosphere containing approximately 5–10% CO₂.

Blood agar

On blood agar, colonies are small and may show characteristic alpha haemolysis with a greenish appearance around the colonies.

High-yield: Pneumococcus is classically associated with alpha haemolysis on blood agar.

Liquid media

The notes describe growth in liquid media and changes associated with bacterial activity.

3. Biochemical Reactions

Important reactions and identification tests described in the notes include:

Test / Reaction Result / Importance
Catalase Negative
Oxidase Negative
Carbohydrate fermentation Sugars are fermented.
Bile solubility Positive / bile soluble.
Optochin Used as an important identification test.

Bile Solubility Test

The PDF describes the bile-solubility test as an important identification method for pneumococcus.

When the appropriate bile salt solution is added, the bacterial cells undergo autolysis and the suspension becomes clear.

4. Pathogenesis

The PDF notes describe pneumococci as organisms that can colonize the human upper respiratory tract.

Colonization

The bacteria can colonize the human nasopharynx and may be carried without causing disease.

Spread

From the respiratory tract, the organism may spread to other sites and cause disease.

Role of capsule

The capsule is an important virulence-associated structure and helps the organism resist host defence mechanisms.

→

→
Invasive disease

5. Diseases Caused

Pneumonia Important respiratory disease caused by pneumococcus.
Meningitis Infection involving the meninges.
Otitis media Infection of the middle ear.
Sinusitis Infection involving the paranasal sinuses.
Bacteremia Presence and spread of bacteria in the bloodstream.
Septicemia Severe systemic infection.

6. Laboratory Diagnosis

1. Specimen

The specimen depends on the site of infection. Blood and respiratory specimens may be required depending on the clinical condition.

2. Microscopy

In suitable specimens, Gram staining can demonstrate Gram-positive diplococci.

3. Culture

The specimen is cultured under appropriate conditions, including suitable atmosphere and temperature.

4. Identification

Gram stain Gram-positive diplococci.
Blood agar Small colonies with alpha haemolysis.
Optochin Useful for identification.
Bile solubility Bile-soluble reaction supports identification.
Capsule detection Capsule can be demonstrated by appropriate methods.
