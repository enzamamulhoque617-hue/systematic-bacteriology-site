TOPIC 02 • NOTES

Pseudomonas

The handwritten notes describe Pseudomonas as a Gram-negative bacillus with characteristic pigment production and important pathogenic potential.

Gram reaction Gram-negative bacillus
Important feature Pigment production
Pathogenic clue Blue-green pus / pigment

1. Morphology

  • Pseudomonas is described as a slender bacillus.
  • The notes give an approximate size of 1.5–5 µm.
  • It is described as non-spore-forming.
  • Some organisms may possess glycocalyx.
  • Motility is associated with the organism.
⭐ Key morphology: Slender Gram-negative bacillus → motile → non-spore-forming.

2. Cultural Characteristics

Ordinary Media

  • The notes state that Pseudomonas grows well on ordinary media.
  • Pigment production is an important cultural characteristic.
  • The notes mention a blue-green pigment.

Nutrient Agar

The notes mention a characteristic metallic sheen associated with growth on nutrient agar.

MacConkey Agar

  • The notes describe pale white colonies.
  • The organism is described as non-lactose fermenting.

Blood Agar

The notes mention production of haemolytic colonies on blood agar.

Culture memory:
Ordinary media → Pigment → Metallic sheen → Non-lactose fermenting

3. Biochemical Reactions

The handwritten notes specifically mention the following biochemical reactions:

⭐ Remember: Catalase + + Oxidase +

4. Pathogenesis

The notes identify Pseudomonas as a pathogenic organism associated with production of a characteristic blue-green pigment.

The pigment is associated with the characteristic blue-green appearance described in the notes.

Pseudomonas → Pigment production → Blue-green appearance

5. Laboratory Diagnosis

Specimen Collection

The notes mention collection of an appropriate fecal sample for examination and culture.

Culture

  • The specimen is cultured on suitable media.
  • Cultural characteristics help in identification.
  • Pigment production is an important identifying characteristic.

Identification

The notes emphasize culture followed by identification.

Diagnosis flow:
Specimen → Culture → Colony characteristics → Biochemical tests → Identification
