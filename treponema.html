TOPIC 01 • SPIROCHETES

Treponema

The handwritten notes discuss Treponema as a delicate spirochete associated with sexually transmitted disease, particularly syphilis.

Group Spirochete
Important species Treponema pallidum
Disease Syphilis

1. Spirochetes

The handwritten notes introduce spirochetes as elongated, motile and flexible bacteria.

  • They are described as having a characteristic spiral form.
  • They are very delicate organisms.
  • Their structure allows characteristic movement.
⭐ Key idea: Spirochetes → elongated + flexible + motile.

2. Morphology of Treponema

  • The notes describe Treponema as a short, slender and spiral-shaped bacterium.
  • It has closely spaced spirals.
  • The organism is described as very delicate.
  • It is difficult to observe clearly by ordinary light microscopy because of its delicate structure.
  • The notes mention that it can be observed by dark-field microscopy.
⭐ Morphology memory: Short + slender + spiral + delicate.

3. Cultural Characteristics

The handwritten notes indicate that Treponema does not grow in ordinary culture media.

Important exam point: Treponema pallidum is not routinely cultivated on ordinary laboratory culture media.

4. Biochemical Reactions

No specific biochemical reactions are clearly recorded in the Treponema section of the uploaded handwritten notes.

For this reason, additional biochemical reactions have not been added from outside sources.

5. Pathogenesis

The notes describe Treponema as an organism that infects humans and is transmitted mainly through sexual contact.

  • Infection can occur through the mucous membrane or skin.
  • After entering the body, the organism multiplies.
  • It can enter the bloodstream and spread through the body.
  • The notes mention acquisition through sexual contact.
  • An incubation period of approximately 3 weeks is mentioned.
Entry through skin / mucosa → Multiplication → Bloodstream → Spread

6. Disease & Clinical Features

Syphilis

Treponema pallidum causes syphilis.

The notes mention a primary lesion / chancre associated with the disease.

Skin and mucous-membrane manifestations are also described in the clinical features.

⭐ Exam line: Treponema pallidum → sexually transmitted infection → syphilis.

7. Laboratory Diagnosis

Specimen

The notes mention collection of material from appropriate lesions for examination.

Microscopy

  • Dark-field microscopy can be used to observe the delicate spirochete.
  • The notes specifically mention dark-field microscopy for demonstration of the organism.

Serological Tests

The handwritten notes mention serological testing.

  • ELISA is mentioned.
  • Other serological tests are also indicated in the notes.
Clinical specimen → Dark-field microscopy + Serological tests → Diagnosis
