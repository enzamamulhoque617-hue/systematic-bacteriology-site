1. Morphology

  • N. gonorrhoeae is a Gram-negative bacterium.
  • It occurs as diplococci.
  • The cells are generally oval or rounded.
  • The cocci occur in pairs.
  • They are non-motile.
  • They do not form spores.
  • The PDF notes describe pili and other surface structures associated with the organism.
Remember: N. gonorrhoeae → Gram-negative diplococci → pairs.

2. Cultural Characteristics

N. gonorrhoeae is a fastidious organism and requires suitable enriched culture conditions.

Growth conditions

  • Growth is favoured around 35–37°C.
  • The notes describe enriched media and conditions suitable for its growth.
  • Colonies are generally small and delicate.

Culture media

Enriched media are required for cultivation. Chocolate agar and selective media are commonly used for isolation.

High-yield: Gonococcus is fastidious and requires enriched culture conditions.

4. Pathogenesis

N. gonorrhoeae causes disease following infection of susceptible mucosal surfaces.

Attachment

The organism attaches to host epithelial cells using surface structures.

Local infection

Infection produces inflammation and purulent discharge at affected sites.

5. Diseases Caused

Gonorrhoea The principal disease caused by N. gonorrhoeae.
Urethral infection Infection of the urethra may produce purulent discharge.
Genital infection The organism can infect the genital mucosal surfaces.
Female genital infection Infection may involve the female genital tract.
Complications Infection may spread beyond the initial site and produce complications.

6. Laboratory Diagnosis

1. Specimen

Specimens are collected from the appropriate infected site. The PDF mentions collecting discharge from the urethra and relevant genital specimens.

2. Microscopy

Gram staining may demonstrate Gram-negative diplococci.

3. Culture

The specimen can be cultured on appropriate enriched media under suitable conditions.

4. Molecular diagnosis

The PDF notes mention molecular diagnosis, including PCR.

Specimen Collect appropriate genital/urethral specimen.
Gram stain Look for Gram-negative diplococci.
Culture Use suitable enriched culture media.
Biochemical tests Oxidase and carbohydrate reactions help identification.
Molecular test PCR-based molecular diagnosis may be used.

7. High-Yield Points

  • Gram-negative diplococci.
  • Non-motile and non-spore-forming.
  • Fastidious organism requiring suitable enriched culture conditions.
  • Oxidase positive.
  • Catalase positive.
  • Ferments glucose but not maltose.
  • Causes gonorrhoea and genital tract infection.
  • Gram stain, culture and molecular methods are useful in laboratory diagnosis.
