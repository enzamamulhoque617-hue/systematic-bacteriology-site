TOPIC 01 • NOTES

Enterobacteriaceae

Enterobacteriaceae are an important group of Gram-negative bacilli. Your handwritten notes cover E. coli, Salmonella and Shigella.

Group Gram-negative bacilli
Organisms in notes E. coli • Salmonella • Shigella
Main importance Intestinal & systemic infections

1. General Characteristics

  • Enterobacteriaceae are described in the notes as Gram-negative bacilli.
  • They are generally non-spore-forming.
  • Some members are motile, while others may be non-motile.
  • They can grow readily on ordinary culture media.
  • The notes mention fermentation of glucose, with acid and/or gas production depending on the organism.
⭐ Remember: Enterobacteriaceae → Gram-negative bacilli → important intestinal pathogens.

2. Escherichia coli

The notes identify E. coli as an important member of Enterobacteriaceae and as part of the normal intestinal flora.

Morphology

  • It is a rod-shaped Gram-negative bacterium.
  • The notes describe a size of approximately 1–3 µm.
  • It is generally non-spore-forming.
  • Flagella may be present and provide motility.

Cultural Characteristics

  • E. coli grows readily on ordinary laboratory media.
  • The notes mention growth at approximately 37°C.
  • On suitable agar media, colonies are described as characteristic of E. coli.
  • On blood agar, the notes mention that some strains may show haemolysis.

Biochemical Reactions

  • The notes mention fermentation of glucose, lactose and maltose.
  • Acid and gas production are associated with carbohydrate fermentation.

Pathogenesis / Diseases

The notes list important disease-producing groups of E. coli, including:

  • Diarrhoeagenic E. coli
  • Urinary tract infections
  • Septicemia
  • Pyogenic infections

Laboratory Diagnosis

  • Collection of the appropriate clinical specimen.
  • Culture of the specimen.
  • Identification based on cultural and biochemical characteristics.

3. Salmonella

Morphology

  • Salmonella are described as Gram-negative rods.
  • The notes mention that they are generally motile.
  • They are described as non-spore-forming.
  • The notes mention absence of capsule in the organism being described.

Cultural Characteristics

  • Optimum pH mentioned in the notes is approximately 6.8–7.2.
  • Optimum temperature is approximately 37°C.
  • The notes describe growth on culture media with characteristic colonies.
  • On MacConkey agar, colonies are described according to their lactose reaction.
  • The notes mention black-coloured colonies on bismuth sulphite agar for the relevant Salmonella growth described.

Biochemical Reactions

  • Salmonella ferment glucose.
  • Acid and gas production are mentioned.
  • The notes state that Salmonella are generally motile.

Pathogenesis / Diseases

The notes describe Salmonella as human and animal pathogens and mention three major infection patterns:

1. Enteric Fever

Includes the typhoid-type illness associated with Salmonella.

2. Gastroenteritis

Food-associated intestinal infection producing gastrointestinal symptoms.

3. Septicemia

Systemic infection in which Salmonella enters the bloodstream.

Laboratory Diagnosis

  • Collection of appropriate clinical specimens.
  • Blood culture is mentioned for enteric fever, followed by relevant testing.
  • The notes also mention PCR as a diagnostic method.

4. Shigella

Morphology

  • Shigella are described as Gram-negative bacilli.
  • They are short rods.
  • The notes describe them as non-motile.
  • They are non-spore-forming.
  • They are described as non-capsulated.

Cultural Characteristics

  • Shigella can grow under both aerobic and facultative anaerobic conditions as described in the notes.
  • The notes mention growth around 37°C.
  • Colonies are described as small, smooth and translucent on suitable media.
  • On MacConkey agar, lactose-negative colonies are associated with Shigella.
  • The notes mention characteristic growth on selective media.

Biochemical Reactions

  • Shigella ferment glucose.
  • The notes describe acid production without gas for the characteristic reaction.
  • Shigella are non-motile.

Pathogenesis / Disease

Shigella infection causes bacillary dysentery / shigellosis.

The notes associate the infection with intestinal disease and inflammatory diarrhoeal illness.

Laboratory Diagnosis

  • Collect the stool specimen without unnecessary delay.
  • Culture the specimen on appropriate selective and differential media.
  • The notes mention enrichment media such as Selenite F broth.
  • Microscopy may be performed, including Gram staining.
  • Serological testing may also be performed as mentioned in the notes.
