TOPIC 01 • NOTES

Corynebacterium diphtheriae

Important Gram-positive bacillus associated with diphtheria. These notes cover morphology, cultural characteristics, biochemical reactions, pathogenesis and laboratory diagnosis.

Type Gram-positive bacillus
Shape Club-shaped
Important disease Diphtheria

1. Morphology

  • Corynebacterium diphtheriae is a Gram-positive bacillus.
  • It is a slender, club-shaped bacillus.
  • The bacilli show an irregularly stained / beaded appearance.
  • The organisms may show characteristic arrangements such as palisade or angular arrangements.
  • The notes describe characteristic intracellular granules that can be demonstrated by suitable staining.
  • The organism is non-motile.
⭐ Important: Remember the combination: Gram-positive + club-shaped + beaded appearance.

2. Cultural Characteristics

  • The organism can grow on ordinary laboratory media.
  • Growth is also described on blood agar.
  • Selective media can be useful for isolation of Corynebacterium from clinical material.
  • Colony appearance should be considered together with microscopic and biochemical findings for identification.
Exam point: Cultural characteristics alone are not enough for final identification; microscopy and biochemical tests are important.

3. Biochemical Reactions

The handwritten notes mention the following biochemical reactions for Corynebacterium:

Test Reaction in the notes
Catalase Positive (+)
Oxidase Negative (−)
Urease Negative (−)
Indole Negative (−)
Glucose fermentation Ferments glucose with acid production, without gas
🧠 Remember:

Catalase → +
Oxidase → −
Urease → −
Indole → −
Glucose → acid, no gas

4. Pathogenesis

The important factor in the pathogenesis of diphtheria is the production of diphtheria toxin.

C. diphtheriae → Diphtheria toxin → Inhibition of protein synthesis → Cell / tissue damage

According to the notes, the diphtheria toxin is a protein toxin. It acts by interfering with protein synthesis in susceptible cells.

Mechanism of action: Diphtheria toxin inhibits protein synthesis, resulting in damage to susceptible tissues.

5. Disease Caused — Diphtheria

Diphtheria is the important disease associated with toxigenic Corynebacterium diphtheriae.

  • Respiratory diphtheria is an important clinical form.
  • A characteristic pseudomembrane may form in the respiratory tract.
  • The pseudomembrane can act as a mechanical barrier to the respiratory passage.
  • Severe disease may be associated with toxin-mediated complications.
⚠️ Important exam point: Diphtheria toxin is responsible for the major tissue damage associated with the disease.

6. Laboratory Diagnosis

A. Specimen

  • The notes mention collection of material from the lesion / throat area.
  • Material from the affected site should be collected appropriately for laboratory examination.

B. Microscopy

  • Prepare a smear from the clinical material.
  • Suitable stains can demonstrate the characteristic bacilli.
  • Special staining may demonstrate metachromatic granules.

C. Culture

  • Culture the specimen on suitable laboratory media.
  • Blood agar and selective media may be used for isolation.
  • Examine the growth and colony characteristics.

D. Biochemical Tests

  • Catalase, oxidase, urease, indole and fermentation reactions can assist identification.

E. Toxin Detection

  • Demonstration of toxin production is important in determining whether the isolate is toxigenic.
Diagnosis flow:
Specimen → Microscopy → Culture → Biochemical tests → Toxin detection
