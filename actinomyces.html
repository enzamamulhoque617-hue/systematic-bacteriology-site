TOPIC 04 • NOTES

Actinomycetes

Actinomycetes are filamentous bacterial forms included in the study of Gram-positive bacilli. The notes mainly discuss their morphology, pathogenicity and laboratory diagnosis.

Group Actinomycetes
Important examples Actinomyces • Nocardia
Important feature Filamentous forms

1. Introduction

The handwritten notes describe Actinomycetes as filamentous bacterial organisms.

The examples written in the notes include:

Actinomyces

An important actinomycete associated with actinomycosis.

Nocardia

Another filamentous bacterial organism included under Actinomycetes in the notes.

2. Morphology

  • Actinomycetes are described as rod-shaped / filamentous bacteria.
  • They may form branching filamentous structures.
  • The notes mention that some organisms are acid-fast.
  • Granules may be associated with the organisms.
  • The notes mention that they are Gram-positive.
  • They are described as non-motile and non-spore-forming.
⭐ Key morphology: Filamentous + branching appearance + Gram-positive nature.

3. Pathogenesis

The notes describe Actinomyces as capable of causing infections when they enter tissues and establish infection.

Actinomycosis is the important disease associated with Actinomyces.

Actinomyces → Tissue infection → Actinomycosis

4. Diseases / Clinical Infections

Your notes specifically mention different sites of Actinomyces infection.

Cervicofacial infection

Infection involving the cervicofacial region.

Thoracic infection

Infection involving the thoracic region.

Abdominal infection

Infection involving the abdominal region.

Remember the sites:

Cervicofacial → Thoracic → Abdominal

5. Laboratory Diagnosis

A. Specimen Collection

  • The notes mention collection of specimens from pus or sputum.
  • Appropriate clinical material should be collected for laboratory examination.

B. Microscopy

  • Microscopic examination is an important part of laboratory diagnosis.
  • The notes mention examination of the organism following appropriate staining.
  • Filamentous/branching bacterial forms can be observed microscopically.

C. Culture / Isolation

  • The notes mention isolation culture.
  • The specimen can be inoculated into suitable culture media.
  • The notes mention incubation around 37°C.
  • Colony growth may take time before it becomes clearly visible.
Diagnosis flow:
Specimen → Microscopy → Culture → Identification
