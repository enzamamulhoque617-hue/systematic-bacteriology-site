1. Introduction

Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM) is an electron microscope. The study notes describe TEM as a microscope in which electrons pass through the specimen to reveal the internal structure.

2. Basic Principle

The basic principle is based on the transmission of electrons through the specimen. The transmitted electrons provide information about the specimen's internal structure.

3. Working Principle

  • TEM uses an electron beam.
  • The electrons pass through the specimen.
  • The transmitted electrons provide information about the specimen.
  • This allows the internal structure to be observed.

4. Special Feature

The important feature of TEM mentioned in the notes is that electrons pass through the specimen, allowing the internal structure to be observed.

5. Electron Microscope

  • Electron microscopes use an electron beam.
  • TEM is a transmission electron microscope.
  • Electrons pass through the specimen in TEM.
  • TEM is therefore associated with observing internal structure.

6. Applications

The uploaded notes specifically support the use of TEM for observing the internal structure of a specimen. Detailed clinical applications are not specified in the uploaded notes, so additional applications have not been added.
