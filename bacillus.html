TOPIC 05 • NOTES

Bacillus

Bacillus are important spore-forming bacilli. The handwritten notes particularly discuss Bacillus anthracis and Bacillus cereus.

Type Spore-forming bacilli
Important species B. anthracis • B. cereus
Major diseases Anthrax • Food poisoning

1. General Morphology

  • Bacillus are described in the notes as rod-shaped bacteria.
  • They are spore-forming.
  • The notes mention both aerobic and anaerobic spore-forming bacilli, with Bacillus associated with the aerobic group.
  • They are described as generally motile, although important exceptions are noted.
  • Different Bacillus species may differ in their cultural and pathogenic characteristics.
⭐ Key point: Bacillus → rod-shaped + spore-forming.

2. Bacillus anthracis

B. anthracis is the important pathogenic Bacillus discussed in the notes and is associated with anthrax.

Morphology

  • The notes describe it as a large pathogenic bacillus.
  • It is associated with capsule formation.
  • Spores are an important characteristic of the organism.

Important virulence factors

Capsule

The notes describe the capsule as a polypeptide material containing poly-D-glutamic acid.

Anthrax toxin

Toxin production is identified as an important factor in the pathogenicity of B. anthracis.

3. Anthrax

Anthrax is the disease caused by Bacillus anthracis.

The notes describe infection as occurring through exposure to infected animals or animal products.

Important forms mentioned

  • Skin / cutaneous anthrax
  • Pulmonary anthrax
  • Intestinal anthrax
B. anthracis → Exposure / entry → Infection → Anthrax

4. Laboratory Diagnosis of B. anthracis

A. Specimen

The notes mention collection of specimens depending on the site of infection, including material from skin lesions and other clinical specimens.

B. Microscopy

  • Demonstration of characteristic Gram-positive bacilli is important.
  • Spores are not demonstrated by ordinary Gram staining in the same way as vegetative cells.

C. Cultural Characteristics

  • The notes describe characteristic colony appearance on culture media.
  • Colonies may appear large and spreading on suitable media.
  • The notes mention characteristic colonies on agar and blood-containing media.

D. Biochemical Reactions

The notes mention carbohydrate reactions and catalase under biochemical testing.

Diagnosis flow:
Specimen → Microscopy → Culture → Biochemical tests

5. Bacillus cereus

Bacillus cereus is discussed in the notes in relation to food poisoning.

  • It can be associated with contaminated food.
  • The notes describe food-related illness following consumption of contaminated food.
  • Different clinical patterns are mentioned, including diarrhoeal and emetic types.

Diarrhoeal type

Associated with diarrhoea and abdominal symptoms following contaminated food.

Emetic type

Associated with vomiting after consumption of contaminated food.

6. Cultural Characteristics

  • The notes describe characteristic colonies on agar plates.
  • Colonies may be large and spreading depending on the organism.
  • Colony morphology is useful during identification.
  • The notes also mention characteristic growth on blood-containing media.
Exam focus: Remember to describe colony appearance when writing the cultural characteristics of Bacillus.

7. Biochemical Reactions

The handwritten notes mention carbohydrate utilization/fermentation and catalase under biochemical reactions.

Test / reaction Point recorded in the notes
Carbohydrate reaction Glucose and other sugars are mentioned under biochemical testing.
Gas production The notes mention acid production without gas in the reaction described.
Catalase Catalase is mentioned under biochemical reactions.

8. Pathogenesis

The notes emphasize virulence factors in the pathogenicity of B. anthracis.

Capsule + Anthrax toxin → Virulence → Anthrax
High-yield: For B. anthracis, remember capsule + toxin as the major pathogenicity points recorded in your notes.
