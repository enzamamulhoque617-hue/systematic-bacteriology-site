TOPIC 05 • NOTES

Bordetella

Bordetella is a Gram-negative bacterial genus. The handwritten notes particularly associate Bordetella with whooping cough.

Gram reaction Gram-negative
Shape Small coccobacillary form
Major disease Whooping cough

1. Morphology

  • The notes describe Bordetella as a Gram-negative bacterium.
  • It is described as small.
  • The notes mention a coccobacillary / short bacillary appearance.
  • The organism is described as non-motile.
  • It is non-sporing.
  • The notes mention that it may be capsulated.
⭐ Key morphology: Small + Gram-negative + non-motile + non-spore-forming.

2. Cultural Characteristics

  • The notes describe Bordetella as an obligate aerobe.
  • Growth is described as occurring around 35–37°C.
  • The notes mention colonies as small, smooth and grey-white.
  • Haemolysis is mentioned in the cultural characteristics.
Culture memory: Obligate aerobe → small, smooth, grey-white colonies.

3. Biochemical Reactions

The handwritten notes describe Bordetella as biochemically inactive.

  • It does not ferment sugars as described in the notes.
  • The notes mention that some strains may produce oxidase.
  • The notes mention catalase as part of the biochemical examination.
Source-based note: The biochemical section of the handwritten notes is brief, so no additional biochemical reactions have been added from outside sources.

4. Pathogenesis

The notes describe Bordetella as an obligate human parasite.

It is associated with whooping cough.

The organism affects the respiratory tract and is responsible for the disease described in the notes.

Bordetella → Respiratory tract → Whooping cough

5. Disease Caused

Whooping Cough

The major disease associated with Bordetella in your handwritten notes is whooping cough.

The disease is a respiratory infection and is associated with characteristic coughing episodes.

⭐ Exam line: Bordetella → respiratory infection → whooping cough.

6. Laboratory Diagnosis

A. Specimen Collection

  • The notes specify collection of respiratory samples.
  • A nasal / respiratory swab is mentioned.

B. Microscopy

  • The notes mention fluorescent microscopy.
  • Fluorescent microscopy can be used as part of laboratory examination.

C. Molecular Diagnosis

The notes mention PCR as a method of laboratory diagnosis.

Diagnosis flow:
Respiratory sample → Microscopy → PCR → Diagnosis
