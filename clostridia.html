TOPIC 03 • NOTES

Clostridia

Clostridia are important Gram-positive bacilli associated with diseases such as gas gangrene, tetanus and botulism.

Type Gram-positive bacilli
Special feature Spore formation
Major diseases Gas gangrene • Tetanus • Botulism

1. Morphology

  • Clostridia are Gram-positive bacilli.
  • They are generally large bacilli.
  • The notes describe them as motile, with some exceptions.
  • Spores are present in many Clostridium species.
  • Spores may be terminal, subterminal or central, depending on the organism.
  • Some species show characteristic shapes because of their spores.
⭐ Key point: Think of Clostridia as Gram-positive, spore-forming bacilli.

2. Cultural Characteristics

  • The notes describe growth under anaerobic conditions.
  • Many species produce characteristic colonies and fermentation products.
  • Saccharolytic species can ferment carbohydrates.
  • Some species produce gas during fermentation.
  • Some organisms produce characteristic odour during growth.
Remember: Clostridia are especially associated with anaerobic growth.

3. Important Clostridium Species

C. perfringens

Associated with gas gangrene and wound infections. The notes describe it as an important species.

C. tetani

Produces tetanus and is associated with tetanus toxin.

C. botulinum

Produces botulinum toxin and causes botulism, particularly after consumption of contaminated food.

4. Pathogenesis

Disease caused by Clostridia is mainly associated with the production of powerful toxins.

A. Exotoxins

Pathogenic Clostridia produce exotoxins that are responsible for important disease manifestations.

B. C. tetani — Tetanus

C. tetani produces tetanus toxin. The notes associate the toxin with the characteristic manifestations of tetanus.

C. C. perfringens — Gas Gangrene

C. perfringens is associated with gas gangrene, particularly in damaged or contaminated tissue.

The notes describe tissue destruction and gas production as important features.

D. C. botulinum — Botulism

C. botulinum causes botulism through consumption of food containing the preformed toxin.

Clostridium → Toxin production → Tissue / nervous effects → Disease

5. Diseases Caused

Organism Important disease Main association in notes
C. perfringens Gas gangrene Wound / tissue infection with gas production
C. tetani Tetanus Tetanus toxin
C. botulinum Botulism Preformed toxin in contaminated food

6. Laboratory Diagnosis

A. C. tetani

  • The notes mention specimen collection for laboratory examination.
  • Microscopy is included in the diagnosis.
  • The notes mention demonstration of characteristic bacilli/spores.

B. C. botulinum

  • Diagnosis can involve examination of the suspected food.
  • The notes mention detection of toxin as part of diagnosis.

C. C. perfringens

  • The notes mention laboratory diagnosis by microscopy and culture.
  • Demonstration of characteristic bacterial features assists diagnosis.
General diagnosis flow:
Clinical specimen → Microscopy → Culture → Identification / toxin detection
