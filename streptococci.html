1. Morphology

  • Streptococci are Gram-positive cocci.
  • They are generally arranged in chains.
  • They may also occur in pairs.
  • Some streptococci may possess a capsule.
  • They are non-motile.
  • They are non-spore-forming.
Remember: Streptococci → Gram-positive cocci → characteristic chains.

2. General Characteristics

Streptococci include organisms that may be normal flora as well as important human pathogens.

The PDF specifically discusses pathogenic streptococci such as S. pyogenes and describes their role in different infections.

Important point

Streptococcal disease depends on the species and the site of infection.

3. Haemolysis on Blood Agar

Streptococci can be differentiated according to their haemolytic reaction on blood agar.

Alpha haemolysis Partial haemolysis producing a greenish appearance around colonies.
Beta haemolysis Complete haemolysis producing a clear zone around colonies.
Gamma haemolysis No visible haemolysis.
Exam point: S. pyogenes is classically associated with beta haemolysis, while pneumococcus shows alpha haemolysis.

4. Pathogenesis

The PDF describes streptococcal infections involving different parts of the body.

Respiratory infection

The pharynx and respiratory tract may be important sites of infection.

Skin and soft tissue infection

S. pyogenes can cause different skin and soft-tissue infections.

Important complications

Certain streptococcal infections may be followed by serious complications.

5. Diseases Caused by Streptococci

Pharyngitis Infection of the pharynx, commonly associated with S. pyogenes.
Tonsillitis Infection involving the tonsils.
Erysipelas A superficial skin infection described in the notes.
Impetigo A contagious superficial skin infection, particularly important in children.
Cellulitis Infection involving skin and soft tissue.
Toxic shock syndrome The notes mention toxic shock syndrome associated with some S. pyogenes strains.
Genital infection Streptococci can be involved in genital infections.
Puerperal fever A serious infection associated with the postpartum period.
Brain infection The notes mention infection of the brain among other infections.
Joint infection Infection of joints may occur.
Kidney complications Certain streptococcal infections may be followed by renal complications.

6. Laboratory Diagnosis

1. Specimen

The specimen depends on the site of infection. The notes mention specimens such as throat swab and pus.

2. Microscopy

Microscopic examination may demonstrate Gram-positive cocci, particularly when a suitable clinical specimen is examined.

3. Culture

Blood agar is used for culture and the haemolytic reaction of colonies can help in identification.

4. Identification

Specimen Collect according to site of infection.
Gram stain Look for Gram-positive cocci.
Blood agar Observe colony morphology and haemolysis.
Biochemical tests Additional tests assist identification.
Identification Correlate morphology, culture and biochemical findings.

7. High-Yield Points

  • Gram-positive cocci.
  • Characteristically arranged in chains.
  • Blood agar haemolysis is an important differentiating feature.
  • S. pyogenes is an important pathogenic streptococcus discussed in the notes.
  • Throat swab and pus are examples of clinical specimens mentioned in the notes.
