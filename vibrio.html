TOPIC 03 • NOTES

Vibrio

The handwritten notes describe Vibrio as a Gram-negative bacillus associated with cholera, with characteristic comma-shaped morphology and growth in an alkaline environment.

Gram reaction Gram-negative bacillus
Shape Short, comma-shaped bacillus
Major disease Cholera

1. Morphology

  • Vibrio is described as a Gram-negative bacillus.
  • The organism has a characteristic short, comma-shaped appearance.
  • The notes mention a size of approximately 1.5–3.0 µm.
  • A flagellum is associated with motility.
  • The organism is described as non-spore-forming.
⭐ Key morphology: Gram-negative + short comma-shaped bacillus + motility.

2. Cultural Characteristics

The notes emphasize that Vibrio can grow well in an alkaline environment.

Optimum pH

The notes mention an optimum pH of approximately 8.2–8.5.

Temperature

The notes mention growth around 37°C as the optimum condition described.

Culture Media

  • The notes mention growth on nutrient agar.
  • Characteristic colonies are described on culture media.
  • Growth on MacConkey agar is also mentioned in the notes.
Culture memory: Vibrio prefers an alkaline environment.

Optimum pH ≈ 8.2–8.5

3. Biochemical Reactions

The handwritten notes mention the following biochemical reactions:

Test / reaction Observation recorded in notes
Carbohydrate fermentation Acid is produced but no gas.
Indole Positive.
Nitrate Positive reaction is mentioned.
Gelatin Gelatin liquefaction occurs; the notes mention the reaction after incubation.
⭐ Quick recall: Acid without gas + Indole positive + Nitrate positive + Gelatin liquefaction.

4. Pathogenesis

The handwritten notes identify Vibrio as the cause of cholera.

Cholera is described as an acute diarrhoeal disease associated with profuse watery diarrhoea.

The notes describe the action of cholera toxin on intestinal cells, resulting in increased fluid and electrolyte loss.

Vibrio → Cholera toxin → Intestinal cells → Watery diarrhoea
Clinical feature: Profuse watery diarrhoea is the characteristic feature emphasized in the notes.

5. Laboratory Diagnosis

A. Specimen

  • Stool is the important specimen mentioned in the notes.
  • The stool specimen should be collected before antibiotic administration.
  • A rectal swab may also be used.
  • The notes mention that the specimen should reach the laboratory promptly for proper isolation.

B. Microscopy

  • The notes mention the use of dark-field microscopy.
  • Phase-contrast microscopy is also mentioned.
  • These methods can be used for examination of the organism in the specimen.

C. Culture

  • The specimen is cultured on suitable media.
  • The notes mention enrichment/culture conditions suitable for Vibrio.
  • Characteristic colony appearance helps in identification.
Diagnosis flow:
Stool / rectal swab → Microscopy → Culture → Identification
